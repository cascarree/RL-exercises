1.1 Sample Efficiency

Q1. How many steps sooner does Dyna-PPO reach 80% of the final PPO return?

Dyna-PPO did not reach the target sooner. Instead, it required
approximately 2,000 additional real environment steps.

-   PPO reached 80% of its final return at 8,000 steps.
-   Dyna-PPO reached the same performance level at 10,000 steps.

Dyna-PPO was approximately 2k steps slower than PPO under the
15k-step training budget.

Q2. Is there an initial “model learning penalty”?

Yes.

During the first 3,000 environment steps, Dyna-PPO consistently
underperformed PPO.

-   PPO average return: 40.5
-   Dyna-PPO average return: 35.3

This indicates an initial model learning penalty, where inaccurate
imagined transitions slow policy learning.

1.2 Model Prediction Accuracy

Q1. Around what MSE threshold does Dyna-PPO begin to outperform PPO?

The one-step prediction error remained essentially zero (MSE ≈ 0.0000)
throughout training. No meaningful MSE threshold could be identified at
which Dyna-PPO began outperforming PPO.

Instead, the slight improvement after approximately 8,000 real
environment steps appears to result from a more diverse replay buffer
rather than lower one-step prediction error.

Q2. How fast does E_k grow, and how does that inform imag_horizon?

The multi-step prediction error increased rapidly due to compounding
errors.

-   E5 ≈ 0.50
-   E10 ≈ 2.72 (5.4× increase)

These results suggest using an imagination horizon of 3–5 steps and
avoiding horizons of 10 or more.

Overall Conclusions:

Dyna-PPO achieved a slightly higher final return (200.6 vs. 196.3), but
learned more slowly and showed only a modest (≈2.2%) improvement on
CartPole-v1. One-step prediction accuracy was excellent throughout
training, while multi-step prediction errors accumulated rapidly,
limiting the usefulness of long imagined rollouts. Overall, CartPole is
too simple to demonstrate a significant advantage from Dyna-PPO.
